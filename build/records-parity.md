**1. What the first (list) shows per record that the second does not, or shows worse**

- **Date:** Full date on every row. The second shows a date on its single sample record only; every other report exists as a bar in a monthly histogram.
- **Operator:** Named per row (Psa Airlines Inc, Ameriflight LLC…). The second's per-aircraft page shows one tail's history only in aggregate; the operator of any individual report is not shown at all.
- **Aircraft type:** Per row, make and model (CNDAIR CL6002D24, BEECH 1900C). The second aggregates types into bar counts; you cannot see which type belongs to which report.
- **Tail:** Per row. (Redundantly, though — the tail appears twice per row, once in the tail column and again under the date.)
- **System / ATA chapter:** Per row, including the chapter number (ch. 53, ch. 57) with the zone. The second gives ATA chapters only as tallies per zone; no chapter is attached to any individual report.
- **Part, what was found, crew did, found by, stage:** All five present on every row, in fixed columns, scannable down the list. The second shows none of these per record — they exist only as the highlighted categories in the headline line of the one sample report.
- **Narrative:** Full mechanic's text inline under every row, with the search term highlighted in context, and a "Read the whole write-up" expander when truncated. The second shows one narrative per page view.
- **Ordering:** Newest first with the tie-break rule stated (control number), so the reader knows where any report sits. The second's sample is explicitly "one report from this selection" — no order, no position.
- **Density and comparison:** Five-plus full records per screen; a reader can compare adjacent rows (same tail, same day, same part) without clicking. On the second, comparing two reports requires leaving the view.
- **Action per row:** "Case sheet" per record plus inline expansion. The second offers one "click to open the full report" link for the sample only.

**2. What the second does better**

- It separates "the FAA's own filing of it" from "the mechanic's words as written" and says so at the record. The first merges everything into one undifferentiated block of monospace; a reader cannot tell filing metadata from the mechanic's prose without inferring it.
- The sample record is a clean deep link ("Click to open the full report →"), one obvious affordance versus the first's flatter hierarchy of Case-sheet buttons and expanders.
- The per-aircraft page offers **Export CSV with a citation header and one row per write-up** — every record, machine-readable, citable. The first exposes nothing comparable in these views.
- It is honest about limits ("counts are of reports filed, not of flights"). The first's rows carry no such caveat — a reader could take "253,910 reports" at face value.
- The first is genuinely wasteful per row: the tail duplicated, four consecutive Psa rows repeating five identical fields each, with only one or two words of difference. The second never repeats itself, because it mostly declines to show records at all — which is exactly the trade.

**3. What a reporter needs in a single row to quote it**

Date, tail, operator, aircraft type, and the verbatim mechanic's text, plus a stable identifier or per-report link for the citation. **The first provides nearly all of it in the row itself** — date, operator, type, tail, system, and full verbatim text, expandable when truncated. What it lacks is a visible control number or report ID, so the reporter is citing fields, not a record, unless the Case sheet supplies one. **The second provides the identifier framing (distinct FAA filing vs. write-up, per-report link, CSV with citation header) but does not put records in front of the reader** — one sample per view, everything else behind an export. For choosing and quoting a specific report from a list, the first wins; for citing the one report it does show, the second's apparatus is stronger.
